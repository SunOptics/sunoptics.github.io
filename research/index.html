Research approach and future directions

My work has one aim: to make the localized states of driven optical systems predictable enough to design. Across nanolasers, multimode fibers, and Kerr resonators I have followed the same object — a self-sustained pulse held together by nonlinearity, loss, and drive — and the same method: reduce a high-dimensional model to the few coordinates that actually move, map the bifurcations that bound stability, then hand experimentalists a boundary rather than an anecdote. What began as separate platforms has converged on a single question: how do added degrees of freedom — extra modes, a second nonlinearity, a phase-sensitive pump, quantum noise — reshape the existence and stability of a soliton?

The next stage takes that question into multimode and hybrid χ⁽²⁾/χ⁽³⁾ resonators, where several fields, modes, and frequencies compete inside one cavity. These platforms promise broadband comb generation, multicolor synchronization, and parametrically driven cavity solitons, and they open instability mechanisms and noise pathways that no single-mode theory covers. I am building the design tools they need: reduced-order models and bifurcation maps that say where a state exists, large-scale simulations that test whether it survives realistic imperfection, and a noise theory that converts those maps into the quantities an experiment actually measures — timing jitter, phase noise, coherence, conversion efficiency. I want models that do not merely explain a measurement afterwards, but point to the operating point worth building.

Brussels · 2024–present

Parametric driving and the soliton noise floor

Parametric driving makes a Kerr cavity phase-sensitive: solitons lock into crystals, and I map the quantum noise floor they cannot beat.

In Brussels I work on parametrically driven Kerr cavities, where the drive imposes a discrete phase symmetry on the intracavity field instead of fixing its phase outright. I showed that this phase sensitivity produces parametric soliton crystals: solitons that settle into regularly spaced, phase-locked arrangements rather than sitting wherever they were written. Ongoing collaborative work extends the same platform to single-pulse parametrically driven cavity solitons, to soliton molecules, and to dual-pumped configurations.

In parallel I quantified the quantum-limited noise of cavity solitons, linking timing jitter, phase–amplitude coupling, and relative intensity noise (RIN) inside a single collective-coordinate description. The point is that these are not independent budgets: the fluctuation channels of a soliton are coupled, and treating them together tells an experiment which observable will limit it first. I also contribute to studies of high-finesse Raman-gain fiber resonators with G. Semaan, which widen the range of cavities in which these dynamics can be tested.

Rome · 2021–2024

Cavity solitons in engineered phase landscapes

Shaping the intracavity phase — parabolic potentials, chirped pumps, an added χ⁽²⁾ — turns a Kerr cavity into a controllable laboratory for localized states.

A coherently driven Kerr cavity supports dissipative structures whose existence and stability depend on the phase landscape the light meets on each round trip, so the landscape itself becomes the control knob. With a parabolic intracavity phase I mapped the coexistence of Kerr solitons, breathers, and chimera or chaoticon states, in which a coherent localized core survives inside a chaotic background. At normal dispersion I identified the multimode-resonance transition that carries the cavity from dark to bright solitons through collapsed snaking, and in three dimensions I found high-order cavity solitons and breathers that persist as robust attractors of the driven-dissipative dynamics.

To connect these regimes rather than catalogue them, I developed reduced-dimensional descriptions that place localized states of different dimensionality on a common footing — a transdimensional view that unifies structures across space–time scales. Alongside this I demonstrated control of solitons and breathers by chirped pumping, and contributed to bistable soliton frequency combs in a second-harmonic-generation Kerr cavity and to the interplay of χ⁽²⁾ and χ⁽³⁾ effects in microcomb generation — the hybrid systems that now anchor my forward programme.

Rome · 2021–2024

Solitons in high-dimensional fiber systems

High-dimensional propagation has its own soliton physics: collisions, temporal reflections, and light bullets that no single-mode picture predicts.

I completed a coherent programme on multimode-fiber soliton physics, combining modeling with experiment to uncover spatiotemporal effects unique to high-dimensional propagation. Multimode solitons do not collide the way single-mode pulses do, because the modal content is redistributed at each interaction; I developed that result from the first collision measurements in graded-index fiber through a book chapter to an invited review of the field. Related work established how a dissipative wave reflects from a soliton — the mechanism behind intermodal all-optical switching and frequency conversion — and characterized multimode solitons in step-index as well as graded-index fiber.

On the theory side I built variational frameworks for three-dimensional spatiotemporal solitons, including a Hamiltonian stability analysis and pure-quartic light bullets in graded-index media; these are the tools I later carried into cavity work. Experiment-facing contributions over the same period covered multiphoton beam ionization in standard fibers, modal phase locking, and the statistical mechanics of beam self-cleaning, where optical thermalization accounts for how a speckled beam reorganizes into a clean fundamental mode.

Paris · 2016–2021

Mode locking at the nanoscale

Mode locking survives in a nanolaser, with the pulse period set by the photonic potential rather than by the cavity length.

My doctoral work asked whether mode locking survives when a laser is shrunk to the scale of its own wavelength. Working with ultra-compact semiconductor nanolasers based on photonic-crystal cavities that support Hermite–Gaussian eigenmodes, I used the Gross–Pitaevskii equation and bifurcation analysis to show that mode locking sets soliton pulses oscillating harmonically inside the photonic cavity.

The outcome reframed three things. The mode-locking period is governed by the photonic potential rather than by the cavity length, so the repetition rate follows from the design of the trap. The device can be steered controllably across Q-switching, continuous-wave, and Q-switched mode-locked operation. And the locked state proves robust against cavity disorder, which I established alongside a noise analysis of continuous-wave and mode-locked harmonic nanolasers — the first form of the noise question that now sits at the center of my programme.
